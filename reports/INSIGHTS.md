## Business problem
An insurer needs to understand **who its costliest policyholders are, why they cost more,
and what it can do about it**, both for fair pricing and for targeted cost-reduction programmes.

## Data
- 2,772 raw records, 1,435 duplicates removed, 0 missing values.
- 1,337 unique policyholders analysed.

## Key insights

1. **Smoking is the #1 cost driver.** Smokers pay **3.8x** more on average
   ($32,050 vs $8,441; Welch t-test p < 0.001, Cohen's d = 3.16).
   They are **20% of customers but 50% of total cost**.

2. **Obesity matters mainly for smokers (interaction effect).** Among smokers, obese members cost
   **$20,195 more** (p < 0.001). Among non-smokers the gap is only
   **$879** (d = 0.15). So a "BMI surcharge" for everyone would be unfair and poorly targeted.

3. **Costs are highly concentrated.** The top 10% of policyholders generate **32%** of total cost,
   and the top 20% generate **52%**.

4. **High + Critical risk tiers** make up **20% of customers** but
   **49% of cost**. Critical-tier members (smoker + obese) average
   **$41,558**.

5. **Age is the main driver for non-smokers** (Pearson r = 0.63 within non-smokers,
   p < 0.001). **Sex** (p = 0.034, but Cohen's d = 0.12)
   and **region** (eta² = 0.007) are statistically weak or practically negligible.

## A/B test: smoking-cessation wellness programme (simulated)
- Power analysis: **205 smokers per group** to detect a 10% cost reduction (alpha = 0.05, power = 0.80).
- Result: treatment changed mean cost by **-7.5%** (95% CI $-4,429 to $-269, p 0.027).
- Saving per member **$2,349** vs programme cost **$1,500**,
  so net **$849/member (ROI 57%)**. Guardrail: engagement 74%.
- **Decision: Roll out.**
